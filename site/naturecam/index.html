The NatureCam project

A live camera at an African waterhole.
And the AI we are building on it.

Djuma Cam is a pan-tilt-zoom (PTZ) camera overlooking Gowrie Dam in the Sabi Sand, South Africa. Volunteer camera operators, known as zoomies, steer it remotely from around the world: they turn it, tilt it and zoom in to follow the animals.

On this camera we are developing three things: a world model, which is an AI understanding of the scene the camera watches; individual re-identification, which recognises animals that return; and an AI co-pilot that helps the operators find and follow animals.

Open to everyone: the live stream on Djuma’s YouTube channel, and every bird call the camera’s microphone records, published with its recording, spectrogram and AI species suggestion on Djuma birds. You can run your own models on the live stream or on those recordings and compare their answers with the published suggestions.

01 / The NatureCam project
01

Observe

Djuma’s camera began sharing images in 1998. Generations of viewers and volunteer camera operators have built a shared record of life at the same waterhole, a history that gives each new observation context.

Watch Djuma Cam ↗
02

Recognise

Hosana is the re-identification pipeline we’re building. Its goal is to connect repeat sightings of the same animal over time, starting at Djuma. Individual recognition is still being researched and validated.

In development
03

Control

Zoomies are the people who remotely pan, tilt and zoom nature cameras. We’re developing an AI zoomie to help them notice activity, follow animals and choose useful views, with human judgement at the centre.
